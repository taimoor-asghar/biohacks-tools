These tools are the opposite of hype: a sleep debt calculator that tells you repayment takes weeks, a biological age estimator that admits it is an estimate, an HRV tracker that warns you single-day swings are noise. Every tool shows its maths, cites its sources, and flags where the evidence is thin. That is what "evidence-first" means here — numbers you can act on, and honesty about what they cannot tell you.

How these tools work

Each tool is a single static page: the calculation happens in JavaScript inside your browser, so nothing you type is sent anywhere. The formulas are published ones — Mifflin–St Jeor for metabolic rate, Hodgdon–Beckett for Navy body fat, Uth–Sørensen–Overgaard for VO2 max — and every tool page explains the maths it uses, shows worked examples, and links the key references.

Equally important is what the tools do not do. They do not diagnose, they do not prescribe, and they do not replace your doctor. Estimates like biological age and deep-sleep minutes are models, not measurements; the pages say so plainly. Where the evidence behind a biohacking claim is thin — and in this space it often is — the guide text says that too.
